II. Translator’s Note: The Bridge of Meaning
This English edition is a condensed conceptual adaptation. It is not a word-for-word translation, but rather an "extraction of essence." It presents the core philosophical and Qur’anic framework in accessible English, omitting the exhaustive linguistic debates and classical references found in the original Arabic text.
For the academic researcher: The original Arabic version remains the primary source for comprehensive linguistic analysis, detailed exegesis (Tafsir), and the complete bibliography.
The five methodological levels are Qur’anic text, linguistic explanation, inference, hypothesis under examination, and operational model. Simplification must preserve their differences. An interpretation is not made certain by being placed beside a verse.
Key terms include baʿth (raising or commissioning, with resurrection in relevant contexts), irsāl (sending), rasūl (messenger), nabī (prophet), balāgh (conveyance), bayān (clarification), ḥukm (judgment), tazkiyah (purification), and ḥamd (praise). Further conceptual readings of these terms belong to the author’s interpretative model.
1 The Epistemic Problematic — Decoupling Text from Historiography
1.1 The Classical Dilemma: Textual Monopoly vs. Reactive Rejectionism
Debates about Qur’anic interpretation and transmitted reports encompass diverse approaches to authenticity, authority, specification, and abrogation. This study examines that relationship without reducing the historical disciplines to a single position or dismissing reports as a class.
The inquiry asks what functions the Qur’an attributes to the Messenger. The author uses this textual inquiry to propose a framework for evaluating reports. The framework remains a human construction whose categories and applications require scrutiny alongside evidence about transmission and context.
1.2 The Five-Tier Epistemic Hierarchy
The following distinctions prevent an interpretation or analytical tool from acquiring the authority of the wording it seeks to explain. Linguistic explanations and inferences may themselves differ in confidence.

1.3 Testing Non Synonymy in Context
The study tests whether related expressions display differences of usage and emphasis. A distinction must emerge from constructions and contexts; it cannot be imposed on every occurrence merely because two roots differ. Counterexamples may narrow or defeat the proposed distinction.
2 The Lexical Networks of Commission (Baʿth) and Dispatch (Irsāl)
2.1 Complete Corpus Induction: 67 vs. 513 Occurrences
The inquiry begins by cataloging every appearance of the roots (b-ʿ-th) and (r-s-l) in the Qur’an as documented in the Quranic Arabic Corpus, establishing an exhaustive empirical baseline:
• The local register records 67 occurrences of b-ʿ-th. Its working categories comprise 38 resurrection-related occurrences, 14 occurrences concerning prophets, messengers or warners, 6 human delegations, 4 dispatches of creatures, 3 awakenings from sleep, and 2 worldly uprisings. These are analytical classifications, not meanings proved by the root alone; borderline cases require their recorded qualifications.
• Root (r-s-l) [513 occurrences]: Distributed across 8 morphological forms: 130 Form-IV verbs (arsala), 332 nominal occurrences (rasūl/rusul), 10 feminine nouns (risālah/risālāt), and 41 active/passive participles (mursil, mursal, mursalāt).
2.2 Semantic Orientations: Internal Emergence vs. Directed Vector
The comparison supports a provisional difference of emphasis in selected constructions, rather than mutually exclusive dictionary meanings.
• In relevant messenger passages, baʿth may foreground raising or commissioning within a community. The working group of 14 is not evidence that every occurrence explicitly states a shared social origin.
• Irsāl can foreground sending toward an addressee or purpose. Its corpus also includes human senders and nonhuman objects; it cannot universally mean an external divine sender carrying revelation.
2.3 Falsification Testing: The Resisting Witnesses
To prevent premature essentialization, the hypothesis was subjected to reverse testing. Two prominent counter-witnesses demonstrate that 'fīhim/minhum' is not the exclusive grammatical property of baʿth:
Textual Falsification Witness
"Then We sent among them a messenger from themselves (fa-arsalnā fīhim rasūlan minhum), saying: Worship God..." (Al-Mu’minūn 23:32)
"Just as We sent among you a messenger from yourselves (kamā arsalnā fīkum rasūlan minkum)..." (Al-Baqarah 2:151)
The counterexamples prevent a strict lexical separation. The proposed contrast concerns contextual emphasis on commissioning and directed sending. Its prevalence and limits require comparison across the classified corpus.
2.4 The Seven Structurally Open Occurrences (Sijill al-Taʿlīq)
The register contains 506 classified occurrences and seven structurally suspended occurrences: 21:5, 28:45, 42:51, 44:5, 77:11, 81:19 and 83:33. Their uncertainties differ, including a general sending description, reported speech, and an unnamed referent. Suspension does not erase contextual evidence: 81:19 must be read with 81:20–25, and 83:33 with 83:29–32. Two additional occurrences, 10:21 and 20:96, are already counted among the 506; their classification is retained while the referent remains open. They do not increase the total.

3.2 The Restriction Formulas: Balāgh as the Delimitation of Responsibility
Restriction formulas include “nothing is upon the Messenger except conveyance” in 5:99, and formulations specifying clear conveyance in 24:54 and 29:18. Their wording and contexts should be distinguished rather than combined into one quotation.
Al-Nūr 24:54 is particularly relevant because it places obedience, differentiated responsibilities and clear conveyance together.
The Structural Key of Al-Nūr: 54
"Say: Obey God and obey the Messenger. But if you turn away, then upon him is only that with which he has been charged, and upon you is that with which you have been charged. And if you obey him, you will be guided. And nothing is upon the Messenger except clear proclamation (al-balāgh al-mubīn)." (Al-Nūr 24:54)
The verse contains both the command to obey and a restriction concerning what is upon the Messenger. The restriction therefore cannot simply cancel obedience, teaching or equitable judgment. Reading it as a distribution of responsibility is a contextual inference.
• The Messenger’s assigned responsibility is considered alongside other passages on conveyance, teaching and judgment. The restriction is not an exhaustive denial of those functions.
• The recipients remain accountable for their response. Arguments concerning noncoercion also require their own textual witnesses; they should not be presented as the sole literal meaning of the restriction formula.
4 Nabī (Prophet) and Rasūl (Messenger) in a Single Persona
4.1 Modes of Syntactic Co-occurrence
Both descriptions apply to one person. Their grammatical combinations permit inquiry into different aspects of description, but grammar alone does not prove separate powers or exclusive functional domains.
• Coordination in 22:52 places messenger and prophet under the shared verb of sending. It invites comparison without establishing a complete functional division.
• The paired descriptions in 19:51 and 19:54 apply to Moses and Ishmael. They demonstrate co-reference, not two persons or independent authorities.
• The combined descriptions in 7:157 refer to a single figure. Their syntactic analysis must be distinguished from further claims about authority.
• The formulation in 33:40 combines Messenger of God with seal of the prophets. Claims beyond those descriptions require additional argument.
4.2 The Exhaustive Census of Explicit Vocations
An exhaustive census of direct nominal vocations across the entire Qur’anic corpus yields a remarkable structural pattern:
• The exact vocative “O Messenger” occurs twice, in 5:41 and 5:67. Their contexts include judgment with equity, conveyance and protection. The wording alone does not establish a general theory of immunity in every act.
• The exact vocative “O Prophet” occurs thirteen times across six surahs. Its contexts include family, community, pledges and conflict. Al-Aḥzāb 33:45 directly addresses the Prophet as sent as a witness, bearer of good news and warner. It resists limiting this description to human administration.
4.3 The Four Tiers of Admonition (ʿItāb)
The selected admonition passages exhibit four forms of address. These are descriptive grammatical groupings, not four grades of epistemic certainty.
• Tier 1: Explicit Vocation with Reproof: "O Prophet! Why do you prohibit what God has made lawful for you..." (Al-Taḥrīm 66:1). Direct situational review.
• Tier 2: Direct Pronominal Address without Title: "May God pardon you! Why did you grant them leave..." (Al-Tawbah 9:43). Direct personal query.
• Tier 3: Indefinite Legislative Maxim: "It is not for a prophet to have captives until he has thoroughly subdued the land..." (Al-Anfāl 8:67). General normative boundary.
• Tier 4: Third-Person Narrative without Explicit Naming: "He frowned and turned away, because there came to him the blind man..." (ʿAbasa 80:1-2).
Limits of the Admonition Sample
The selected passages do not use the exact Messenger vocative in their admonitions. This observation is not an exhaustive proof of immunity or a complete division between prophetic and messenger functions.
4.4 The Network of Obedience: Absolute Proclamation vs. 'Fī Maʿrūf'
The text repeatedly commands obedience to God and the Messenger. The absence of the exact imperative “obey the prophet” does not by itself negate obedience to the person described as prophet, or prove that obedience is restricted to recitation.
The pledge in 60:12 includes the qualification fī maʿrūf. Its immediate setting matters when considering broader implications.
The Qualification in the Pledge
"...and that they will not disobey you in any recognized good (wa-lā yaʿṣīnaka fī maʿrūf)..." (Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:12)
The qualification concerns recognized good in the pledge. A universal division between an absolute Messenger domain and a conditional Prophet domain does not follow from this single formulation.
4.5 The Seal of Prophethood and the Limits of Inference
Al-Aḥzāb 33:40 states that Muhammad is not the father of any of the addressed men and describes him as Messenger of God and seal of the prophets.
The study affirms the textual description of the seal of the prophets. Theories about hereditary religious authority, institutional mediation or every form of human immunity must be argued separately; they are not all the direct wording of this verse.

5.3 Solving the Criterion Problem: Guarding Against Selectivity
The protocol seeks to reduce selective acceptance through explicit safeguards. It does not by itself solve interpretative bias or guarantee consistent results.
• Declared Sample: Identify the four reports and the reasons for choosing them before judging their implications; do not claim statistical representativeness.
• 2. Independent Question Axes: Never allowing isnād soundness to substitute for matn criticism, nor lexical elegance to prove transmission veracity.
• 3. Strict Epistemic Grading: Distinguishing between demonstrated historical probability and normative legislative deduction.
• 4. Reverse Testing (Falsification): Actively hunting for resisting reports that challenge the initial hypothesis.
• 5. Transparent Audit Trail: Openly declaring what has been verified, what remains tentative, and what requires further archival investigation.
6 Synthesis — A Conclusion Strictly Graded by Evidence
6.1 The Three Epistemic Tiers of the Research
The synthesis distinguishes direct textual statements, contextual inferences, and unfinished investigation. This three-part presentation summarizes rather than replaces the five methodological levels.
Tier 1: What is Definitively Established by Direct Qur’anic Text (Al-Maqṭūʿ bihi Naṣṣan)
• Textual witnesses: The passages cited contain commands to obey, statements about conveyance, and references to teaching and judgment. Making the Qur’an the governing criterion is the study’s methodological commitment; individual applications remain open to examination.
• Al-Nūr 24:54 explicitly places obedience beside clear conveyance and differentiated burdens. The wider account of responsibility draws on contextual interpretation and additional passages.
• Al-Aḥzāb 33:40 contains the description “seal of the prophets.” It does not explicitly state every institutional consequence proposed by later interpretation.
• Al-Māʾidah 5:67 speaks of protection from people in a conveyance context. The scope of any broader immunity claim requires a separate argument.
Tier 2: What is Preponderant through Inductive Synthesis (Al-Rājiḥ Istinbāṭan)
• Aspectual Differentiation: Contexts suggest differences of emphasis between the descriptions messenger and prophet. Their overlap, particularly 33:45, prevents a complete functional separation.
• Responsibility Distribution: Reading the restriction in 24:54 as delimiting assigned responsibility is supported by its combination with obedience. It remains an interpretative formulation.
• Abrogation and Specification: The no-bequest sample does not settle their relationship. A final judgment requires the transmission variants, the relevant textual arguments and documented historical positions.
Tier 3: What Remains Open to Continued Investigation (Al-Maftūḥ Baḥthiyyan)
• Suspended Classification: Preserve the seven structural suspensions and separately identify the two classified occurrences with open referents, while weighing contextual evidence.
• Transmission Inquiry: Further work includes parallel routes, wording variants, criticism of defects and narrators, and direct comparison with reliable editions and manuscripts where available.
• Reference Verification: Some page references were previously recorded; remaining edition and page matches must be marked individually. Announced future resources must not be represented as already incorporated evidence.
6.2 The Civilizational Horizon: Conscientious Emulation and Moral Agency
Conscientious following joins attention to context with moral responsibility. Distinguishing historically particular actions from broader obligations requires evidence; it cannot justify dismissing established ritual practice through unspecified appeals to purpose.
The judgment report illustrates a limited but significant warning: an outward judgment does not make another person’s right lawful to the claimant. That warning does not settle every judicial procedure. Learning from scholars remains compatible with examining their reasons and sources.
6.3 The Researcher’s Covenant: Humility before the Divine Word
This work is a revisable human inquiry. If every interpretation of the Qur’an is made certain, the researcher’s reading itself acquires an immunity it has not earned. The categories, models and conclusions must remain answerable to evidence.
The Concluding Covenant
"Say: Produce your proof if you are truthful." (Al-Baqarah 2:111)
