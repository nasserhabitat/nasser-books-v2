Introduction: The Question That Begins Before the Model
Look up.
Every human being begins with the same experience: a sky above, an earth beneath, light and darkness, stars appearing at night, the Sun crossing the day, and a horizon separating what is near from what seems unreachable.
The Qur'an repeatedly asks us to look again.
Not merely to see the heavens, but to consider them.
Not merely to count their objects, but to recognize their signs.
Not merely to ask how the universe works, but to ask what kind of order it reveals.
The question of this book therefore begins neither with a telescope nor with a cosmological theory.
It begins with a text.
What kind of cosmos does the Qur'an construct when its cosmic vocabulary is read as an interconnected system rather than as a collection of isolated descriptions?
The Crisis of Reading
Modern readers approach the Qur'anic cosmic verses carrying a pre-existing model in their minds. This is not a neutral act. It shapes—and often distorts—what the text is allowed to say.
We can identify three dominant approaches today:
1. The Harmonizing Reading
This attempts to map modern scientific theories (the Big Bang, relativity, cosmic expansion) directly onto Qur'anic verses. The text becomes a servant to contemporary physics. When theories change, the interpretation must change with them.
2. The Defensive Reading
This insists on an alternative model—sometimes a flat earth, sometimes a geocentric cosmos—and interprets everything through that lens. The text is forced into a rigid framework that often confuses functional description with geometric assertion.
3. The Symbolic Reading
This treats cosmic descriptions as pure metaphors, stripping them of any material significance. The heavens become "spiritual states," the earth becomes "the human heart," and the verses lose their connection to the observable world.
Each of these readings shares the same error.
They begin with a model and then search the text for confirmation.
This book proposes another way: Structural Reading.
Part One: The Methodological Foundation
The Jurisprudence of the Qur'anic Tongue
Before we ask what the sky is made of, we must ask how the Qur'an itself defines and relates its cosmic vocabulary. This requires a methodological shift—a Fiqh al-Lisān al-Qur'āni (The Jurisprudence of the Qur'anic Tongue)—resting on four principles:
1. The Denial of Synonymy
In the Qur'an, no two words perform the exact same function. Samā' (sky) is not samāwāt (heavens). Najm (star) is not kawkab (planet). 'Urūj (ascension) is not ṣu'ūd (climbing). Each word carries a distinct structural role.
2. Dynamic Meaning
The Qur'anic word is not a static noun to be looked up in a dictionary. It is an act and a relationship. Its meaning is determined by its position within a network and its connections with other concepts.
3. The Mathānī: Relational Pairs and Complementary Structures
The Qur'an operates through integrated dualities: heaven and earth, descent and ascent, night and day, light and darkness. These are not opposing contradictions but complementary poles that structure the cosmic order. The term Mathānī (mentioned in Q. 15:87) points to this fundamental architectural principle.
4. Complete Induction Over a Closed Corpus
No concept is understood from a single occurrence. Every term must be studied across all its occurrences in the entire closed Qur'anic corpus (C0: Corpus Closure). This prevents selective reading and ensures that the structural relationships emerge from the text itself, not from the reader's prior assumptions.
The Six-Level Epistemic Matrix (L1–L6)
To prevent epistemological confusion, we introduce a rigorous scale that determines what kind of claim we are making at each stage of interpretation.

The Purpose of the Method:
The purpose of the method is not to maximize interpretation, but to regulate the conditions under which interpretation is permitted.
Part Two: The Architecture of the Heavens
The Sky Is Not Emptiness
Opening Question
What if the sky is not presented to us as emptiness, but as protection?
Qur'anic Passage
"And We made the heaven a protected ceiling" (Q. 21:32).
First Observation
When the Qur'an calls the heaven a protected ceiling, it does something more than describe what appears above the human observer. It introduces a different way of seeing the cosmos. A ceiling is not merely something that exists above us; it is something that covers, bounds, and protects.
The Hidden Relationship
The word protected adds another dimension. The heavens are not presented as an indifferent backdrop against which existence happens. They appear within a system of preservation, held by divine power from falling or collapsing.
Structural Discovery
The Qur'an uses three verbs to describe the construction of the heavens:
Verb
Meaning
Function
Raf' (Raising)
Vertical dimension and elevation
Establishes height and transcendence
Binā' (Building)
Geometric order and structural integrity
Establishes system and coherence
Taswiyah (Leveling)
Symmetry and harmony
Establishes balance and perfection
The heavens are not an accidental void. They are an engineered structure.
Methodological Boundary
This does not, by itself, determine the physical mechanism of that protection. The text establishes a functional description. The physical explanation remains an open field of investigation.
What This Changes
We no longer ask only: What is the sky made of?
We first ask: What does the Qur'an say the sky does? What relation does it establish with the earth? What does it protect? What boundaries does it possess?
This is the beginning of structural reading.
Before asking what a cosmic element is, we ask what role it performs within the system.

The Qur'an uses two forms:

The distinction is not merely grammatical.
The singular points to what is immediately above us—the visible canopy. The plural points to the entire layered architecture of the cosmos. Each layer, according to Q. 41:12, receives its own command (amr). The heavens are functionally differentiated, not identical copies of one another.
The Seven Heavens: When Number Becomes Architecture
Opening Question
Why does the Qur'an repeatedly emphasize the number seven in connection with the heavens?
Qur'anic Passage
"Who created seven heavens in layers; you see no discrepancy in the creation of the Most Merciful. Return your gaze—do you see any fissure?" (Q. 67:3).
Structural Discovery
The number seven is not merely a quantitative count. It appears with accompanying descriptors:
Ṭibāqan (layers, superimposed)
Shidādan (strong, firmly constructed)
Ṭarā'iqa (paths, ways)
These descriptors point to a structural principle rather than a simple arithmetic fact. Seven here indicates completeness, organized multiplicity, and architectural integrity.
Methodological Boundary
The text does not specify the exact nature of these layers. Whether they correspond to atmospheric layers, cosmic realms, or spiritual domains is not determined by the text itself. The functional reality of layered structure is established; the physical identity of those layers remains open.
What This Changes
When the Qur'an speaks of seven heavens, it is not offering a scientific taxonomy. It is inviting us to perceive the cosmos as multi-layered, integrated, and complete—a structure that holds together without fissure or discrepancy.
Part Three: The Components of the Heavenly System
Stars: Sign, Location, and Function
Opening Question
What makes a star more than just a distant light?
Qur'anic Passage
"And landmarks, and by the star they are guided" (Q. 16:16).
"I swear by the locations of the stars" (Q. 56:75).
First Observation
The Qur'an does not describe stars in terms of their physical composition—their mass, temperature, or chemical elements. It describes them functionally.
The Hidden Relationship
Stars appear within three structural roles:
The Sign ('Alāmah): They are points of guidance in darkness.
The Location (Mawqi'): Their value is tied to their positions. The oath is not upon the stars themselves but specifically upon their locations—making position itself a significant structural variable.
The Function: They serve as guidance, adornment, and preservation (stoning the devils).
Structural Discovery
The stars are not merely luminous bodies. Within the Qur'anic network, they function as structural signs associated with guidance, location, and preservation. Their value lies not in their substance but in their position and role.
Methodological Boundary
This does not deny the physical reality of stars. It simply directs our attention to what the text emphasizes: their function within the system of signs, rather than their material composition.
What This Changes
When we read the stars through a structural lens, we stop asking "What are they made of?" and start asking "What do they signify? How do they guide? What does their location tell us about the order of the heavens?"
The cosmos becomes a text of signs, not just a collection of objects.
The Sun and the Moon: Two Lights, Two Functions
Opening Question
Why does the Qur'an describe the Sun and the Moon differently?
Qur'anic Passage
"And He made the Sun a luminous lamp (sirāj) and the Moon a light (nūr)" (Q. 71:16).
First Observation
Two words. Two descriptions. One distinction.
The Hidden Relationship

Self-sourced light; determines time and seasons
The Moon

Structural Discovery
The Sun is described through the functional imagery of a self-illuminating source, whereas the Moon is associated with nūr and with ordered phases (manāzil). This distinction aligns with physical reality without being a technical statement of physics.
Methodological Boundary
We do not claim the Qur'an has "revealed modern astrophysics." We observe that its descriptive precision is functional and structural, not merely poetic.
What This Changes
The Sun and the Moon are not just celestial bodies. They are instruments of time, markers of order, and signs of measured proportion. Their movement is not accidental but calculated—part of a system of ḥisbān (reckoning).

Night and day are not merely physical phenomena. They are signs (āyāt).

Night and day are not just alternations of light and darkness. They form a guidance system. The rhythm of rest and activity, of veiling and revealing, of stillness and movement, is part of a larger order that calls humanity to reflection, gratitude, and measured living.
What This Changes
Time is not a neutral container. It is a structured field of signs. The day calls us to action; the night calls us to reflection. Together, they teach us the rhythm of existence—a rhythm that leads to recognition of the One who ordains it.
Part Four: The Lexicon of Ascending Motion
Motion Is Never Mere Movement
Opening Question
What does it mean to move upward in the Qur'anic cosmos?
First Observation
The Qur'an uses distinct vocabulary for upward motion. These are not synonyms. Each term carries a specific structural function.
The Hidden Relationship
1. 'Urūj (Ascension)
Organized upward movement along a path. It has a specific agent (angels, the Spirit, or humans under specific conditions). It possesses direction and, in some contexts, a temporal dimension. It does not inherently carry a connotation of hardship.
2. Raqī (Elevation/Climbing)
Upward motion connected to a cause or means (sabab). It appears in contexts of request or challenge. Associated with the human agent. It does not carry a connotation of hardship or boundary-transgression.
3. Ṣu'ūd and Taṣa'ud (Climbing and Toiling Upward)
Form
Meaning
Ṣa'ida (simple)
Neutral spatial climbing
Aṣ'ada (IV)
Climbing in a dynamic context
Taṣa'uda (V)
Climbing with hardship and constriction
Ṣu'ūdan / Ṣa'adan
Description of torturous, ascending punishment
Ṣa'īd
Earthly ground or surface
4. Nafādh (Permeation/Breaching)
Motion concerning boundaries: "from the regions of the heavens and the earth" (Q. 55:33). It is conditional upon sulṭān (sovereign authority). Attempting it without authority results in a fiery deterrent (shawāẓ and nuḥās).
Structural Discovery
Motion = Relation + Path + Condition.
To move is to enter into a relationship—with a source, a direction, a cause, and a boundary.
Methodological Boundary
The text does not specify the physical geometry, speed, or mechanism of these motions. It establishes their structural conditions and their functional significance.
What This Changes
We no longer look only at where something moves, but at what makes its movement possible, what governs it, and what its movement accomplishes.
To move, in the Qur'anic vision, is to enter into a relationship.
The Ma'ārij: The Ascending Ways
Opening Question
What are the Ma'ārij mentioned in the Qur'an?
Qur'anic Passage
"From God, Possessor of the Ascending Ways (Ma'ārij)" (Q. 70:3).
First Observation
This is a single occurrence. The word is a plural noun from the root `-r-j, referring to structures or paths of ascent.
Structural Discovery
The Ma'ārij are attributed to God. They are connected directly to the verb ta'ruju (ascend) in the next verse: "The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a day whose measure is fifty thousand years" (Q. 70:4).
The relationship is strong: the Ma'ārij are paths or structures of organized ascent.
Methodological Boundary
The text does not determine whether these are physical stairways, spiritual degrees, cosmic pathways, or something else. The functional relationship is established; the ontological identity remains open.
What This Changes
Even where the text speaks of ascent, it does not reduce it to a physical trajectory. It frames it within a structure of order—a path, a way, a ma'raj. The cosmos, even in its upward motion, is never without structure.
Part Five: The Seven Structural Laws of the Cosmic System
1. The Law of Duality
Why the Cosmos Never Stands Alone
The cosmos is built upon integrated poles: heaven and earth, descent and ascent, night and day, light and darkness. These are not opposing contradictions but complementary structures that hold the system together.
The universe is not a single block. It is a fabric of relationships.
2. The Law of Strata (Layers)
Why Multiplicity Is Not Chaos
The cosmos possesses multiple superimposed levels: heavens in layers, darknesses above one another, light upon light. Each layer has its own function, and together they form an integrated whole without fissure or discrepancy.
The cosmos is not flat. It is layered.
3. The Law of the Sign
Why Everything Points Beyond Itself
The cosmos is a network of indicating signs. Every element within it is an āyah (sign) and a 'alāmah (landmark). The stars, the earth, the alternation of night and day—all are signs that call the observer to recognize the One who ordains them.
Nothing in the cosmos exists only for itself.
4. The Law of Motion
Why Movement Is Never Mere Movement
Motion is a composite phenomenon: Relationship + Path + Condition. It is not blind motion. It is structured, governed, and purposeful.
To move is to enter into a relationship.
5. The Law of Cause
Why Action Requires Mediation
Operational motion requires a network of causes and mediators—material, authoritative, epistemological, and internal. Nothing moves without a cause, and no cause is self-sufficient.
The cosmos is a web of causes, not a collection of isolated events.
6. The Law of Boundaries
Why Possibility Is Never Without a Limit
Cosmic possibility is tied to boundaries and conditions. Permeation requires authority. Ascent requires paths. The heavens have gates, regions, and protections. Boundaries are not prisons; they are the very condition of possibility.
Without boundaries, there is no order. Without order, there is no cosmos.
7. The Law of Function
From Asking "What Is It?" to Asking "What Does It Do?"
The value of an element within the system is determined by its function and relationships, not by its substance or form. The sun is defined by what it does. The stars are defined by what they signify. The earth is defined by what it sustains.
Form follows function. Substance serves relationship.
The Composite Model
The Composite Model = Duality + Strata + Sign + Motion + Cause + Boundaries + Function
This is not a closed theory. It is a structural framework for reading the Qur'anic cosmos.
Part Six: The Internal Cosmos — Functional Correspondence
The Outer and the Inner
Qur'anic Passage
"We will show them Our signs in the horizons and in themselves" (Q. 41:53).
First Observation
The Qur'an places signs in two fields: the outer world (āfāq) and the inner human being (anfus).
The Hidden Relationship
The correspondence is functional, not ontological. The external cosmos is not reduced to an allegory of the inner self. Rather, the Qur'anic discourse allows certain structural relationships observed in the external domain to illuminate corresponding processes within human consciousness.

Methodological Boundary
Functional correspondence does not entail ontological identity.
The sky is not "really" the heart. The earth is not "really" the self. They belong to different domains. But the structural logic—the way they receive, transform, signify, and relate—exhibits patterns that connect the outer and the inner.
What This Changes
The Qur'an does not force us to choose between the outer cosmos and the inner human being.
It teaches us to observe the signs in both.
When we see the stars in the sky, we are reminded of the inner lights that guide us through darkness. When we feel the earth beneath our feet, we are reminded of the ground of certainty that must be established within our hearts. The universe becomes a mirror—not by reduction, but by structural resonance.
Part Seven: Testing Cosmological Models
What the Text Affirms and What It Leaves Open
One of the greatest contributions of structural reading is its ability to distinguish between what the Qur'an actually establishes and what it leaves for human inquiry.

Seven heavens in layers.
The earth is a cradle and a bed.

The existence of regions (aqṭār) and gates (abwāb).
The heavens are held from falling.

The Sun and Moon serve temporal order.

The geometric shape of the earth (spherical or flat).
The physical motion of the earth (rotation or stability).
The exact age of the universe.
The physical mechanism of holding (imsāk).

The physical identity of the Ma'ārij.

Conclusion: The Quran Does Not Depict a Static Cosmos; It Reveals a System
The Final Summary
The Quran does not present a closed astronomical model. It constructs a network of relationships, laws, functions, motions, and boundaries. It leaves to the human intellect the task of discovering the details of the model and testing them—provided that the text is not subordinated to the model, nor the interpreter imprisoned within a historical or contemporary framework.
The Complete Path
Text → Induction → Semantic Network → Relationship → Structural Law → Interpretive Model → External Testing
The Comprehensive Methodological Rule
The model shall not precede the text.
The hypothesis shall not be elevated to the rank of law.
The structural law shall not be transferred to a physical model except through independent proof.
The Invitation
The Quran is not a book of physics. It is not a book for establishing any particular cosmological model.
It is a book of guidance and governance.
It presents a network of relationships, functions, and universals. It remains silent on the geometric and physical details that are left to human inquiry.
The cosmological model most harmonious with the text is the one that preserves the textual universals, leaves the open domain for research, and does not present itself as a substitute for the text or as its sole interpretation.
In One Final Sentence
The Qur'an builds a cosmic system of relationships, laws, and functions, leaving the details of physical models to the human intellect—with the condition that the model does not precede the text, that the hypothesis is not elevated to the rank of law, and that the structural law is not transferred to a physical model except through independent proof.
