III. The Victory Engineering Series
The title “Victory Engineering” may initially suggest a method for producing victory. That is not what this series claims.
The word “engineering” is used here in a structural sense. It refers to the study of relationships, constraints, stages, dependencies, and feedback mechanisms through which human action and human knowledge are formed and evaluated.
The series therefore does not promise victory, nor does it claim to possess a mechanical formula capable of producing it.
The first volume addresses the knowledge side of the project.
The second volume addresses the action side.
The distinction is fundamental:
Volume One:
QCROS — Governing the Production of Qur’anic Knowledge
Volume Two:
Engineering Support — From Governing Action to Reading Outcomes
The relationship between the two volumes is not repetition but methodological succession.
The first volume asks:
How can we control the transition from Qur’anic wording to observation, from observation to hypothesis, from hypothesis to testing, and from testing to a properly ranked conclusion?
The second volume asks:
Once knowledge has been disciplined, how can human action, responsibility, available capacity, interaction, and outcomes be governed without claiming ownership of the final result?
This distinction is expressed in the central principle of the series:
We engineer support and responsible action; we do not engineer victory itself.
IV. Introduction: From Interpreting Statements to Governing the Production of Knowledge
The contemporary problem of engaging with the Qur’an is not simply a shortage of interpretations.
There is already an enormous accumulated body of commentaries, linguistic studies, theological arguments, philosophical readings, legal traditions, and modern interpretations.
The deeper problem is methodological:
How does a researcher move from a Qur’anic observation to a conclusion, and what justifies assigning that conclusion to the Qur’an itself?
This question is often left implicit.
A researcher may begin with a Qur’anic word, propose a possible meaning, connect that meaning to a conceptual pattern, derive a general rule, and finally present the resulting model as though the entire chain had the same epistemic status.
QCROS begins by separating these stages.
There is a fundamental difference between saying:
“This is stated in the Qur’an.”
and saying:
“This is what I observed in the Qur’anic corpus.”
and then:
“This is a hypothesis explaining the observation.”
and finally:
“This appears to be a recurring law within the tested corpus.”
These statements are not equivalent.
The first concerns textual evidence.
The second concerns observation.
The third is a human hypothesis.
The fourth is a higher-level claim that requires broader evidence and resistance testing.
When these levels are merged, a hypothesis can become a “fact,” a model can become “Qur’anic meaning,” and a useful analogy can become an asserted identity.
QCROS is designed to prevent this inflation.
Its fundamental question is therefore not merely:
“What does this word mean?”
but:
“How did we arrive at this meaning, what evidence supports the transition, and what level of claim does that evidence actually justify?”
This is what the project calls claim governance.
Claim governance does not suppress reflection. It protects reflection from premature closure.
Reflection may generate possibilities. Governance determines which possibilities have earned the right to be treated as conclusions.
V. The Core Epistemic Chain
The central architecture of QCROS can be expressed as a controlled sequence:
Qur’anic statement
→ Textual record
→ Observation
→ Observed relationship
→ Hypothesis
→ Test
→ Judgment
→ Operational law
→ Model
Each transition requires its own justification.
The researcher is not permitted to jump directly from a word to a universal theory merely because the final theory appears elegant, coherent, or useful.
This distinction is especially important in Qur’anic linguistic research.
A word, a grammatical form, a root, a recurring pairing, or a structural resemblance may open a question. It does not automatically answer the question.
Similarly, a model may perform successfully in practice. Its practical usefulness does not by itself prove that the model is identical to the Qur’anic meaning from which it was inspired.
QCROS therefore maintains a strict distinction between:
the Qur’anic reference
and
the human construction built around that reference.
VI. The Six Foundational Assumptions
The first chapter of the Arabic volume establishes a set of foundational assumptions that govern the entire system.
1. Coherence of the Qur’anic Discourse
QCROS begins from the methodological assumption that an apparent contradiction in the Qur’anic corpus should first lead the researcher to reconsider the interpretation, the context, the classification, or the relation between the statements.
The researcher should not begin by treating the Qur’anic text itself as the source of the contradiction.
This does not mean that every difficulty already has a known solution.
It means that unresolved tension should remain an unresolved research problem until the relevant evidence has been examined.
A contradiction should not be declared merely because two readings have not yet been reconciled.
2. The Whole Reference
The Qur’an is treated as a single textual reference system.
A statement should therefore not be isolated from the broader corpus when the claim being made concerns the corpus as a whole.
At the same time, unity of reference does not mean uniformity of function.
Two statements may belong to the same reference while performing different functions.
The principle can therefore be expressed as:
Unity of reference does not imply identity of function.
3. Network Priority over the Isolated Occurrence
A single occurrence may reveal a local function, but it is normally insufficient to establish a universal definition.
A Qur’anic expression exists within relationships:
its immediate context,
its grammatical structure,
its associations,
its contrasts,
its recurrence,
its domain,
and its relations to other expressions.
The network therefore does not replace the local context.
Rather:
Context determines local function; the network tests whether that function can legitimately be generalized.
The broader the claim, the broader the corpus required to test it.
4. Corpus Sufficiency Before Closure
A researcher cannot claim that a concept has been established comprehensively until the relevant corpus has been identified and examined in proportion to the scope of the claim.
There is an important distinction between:
extraction completeness
and
semantic completeness.
A researcher may successfully locate every occurrence matching a defined textual pattern and still not have exhausted all possible semantic relationships surrounding those occurrences.
Therefore:
Complete data collection is a condition for testing; it is not a substitute for interpretation or proof.
The larger the claim, the larger and more carefully defined the testing corpus must be.
5. Separation of Epistemic Levels
QCROS insists on distinguishing:
the textual statement,
the observation,
the hypothesis,
the test,
the judgment,
the law,
and the operational model.
This prevents a human construction from silently acquiring the authority of the original text.
The distinction is not merely terminological.
It is a protection against epistemic inflation.
6. Human Results Must Remain Revisable
Every human conclusion remains open to revision when a valid counterexample, methodological defect, or stronger evidence appears.
The researcher is therefore not required to protect a preferred theory at all costs.
A strong methodology must make failure visible.
The ability to say:
“This hypothesis did not survive the test.”
is treated as a scientific achievement rather than a defeat.
VII. Functional Differentiation
One of the important methodological conclusions of the volume is the refusal to assume either universal synonymy or universal absolute differentiation.
QCROS does not begin with:
“These two words are synonyms.”
Nor does it begin with:
“These two words can never overlap.”
Instead, it asks whether functional equivalence or functional differentiation can actually be demonstrated within the relevant corpus.
The governing principle is:
Separate before equating; test before differentiating; and attribute to the text only what the corpus supports.
This is particularly important when studying expressions that appear close in meaning.
Similarity does not automatically establish identity.
Difference does not automatically establish absolute opposition.
The researcher must investigate the actual relations.
For example, two expressions may:
overlap in one context,
diverge in another,
perform complementary functions,
operate at different levels,
or belong to different domains.
The goal is therefore not to impose either sameness or difference, but to determine the type and limits of the relationship.
VIII. The Evidence Ladder
QCROS distinguishes the levels of evidence used in a research claim.
A textual statement is not equivalent to an observation.
An observation is not equivalent to a hypothesis.
A hypothesis is not equivalent to a demonstrated law.
A model is not equivalent to the Qur’anic text.
This produces an epistemic ladder in which every claim must remain at the level justified by its evidence.
The system uses five principal claim states:
Rank
Meaning
A
Textually established within its defined scope
B
Strong systemic result that has passed the specified tests
C
Plausible hypothesis requiring further evidence
D
Deferred or not yet closable
R
Rejected because of a valid counterexample or foundational defect
An important feature of this system is that D is not failure.
A conclusion of “not yet established” may be methodologically stronger than a premature conclusion of certainty.
The system therefore protects the sentence:
“We do not know yet.”
because this sentence preserves the possibility of future research.
IX. Corpus, Context, and Network
QCROS distinguishes between several levels of linguistic analysis.
The basic textual occurrence is the raw unit.
A morphological or syntactic analysis is a research layer.
A root-based classification is another analytical layer.
The system therefore refuses to let a root definition silently become the definition of every form attributed to that root.
The principle is:
Sharing a root opens a field of comparison; it does not prove functional identity.
Likewise, repetition does not automatically establish meaning.
A frequent meaning may be dominant without being universally applicable.
A rare occurrence may contain a restriction that exposes the limits of an overly broad definition.
The network therefore performs several functions:
It tests whether a proposed function recurs.
It identifies resistant cases.
It reveals distinctions.
It tests the possibility of generalization.
It prevents the researcher from defining the whole system from a single occurrence.
But the network itself is not an automatic meaning generator.
It is one of several filters within QCROS.
X. Corpus Sufficiency and Independent Testing
One of the most important methodological principles in the volume is the distinction between material used to construct a hypothesis and material used to test it.
QCROS proposes three environments:
C_build — material used to construct the hypothesis.
C_test — material used to test the hypothesis independently.
C_challenge — material used specifically to attempt to break the hypothesis.
A fourth record, C_resistance, preserves difficult or resistant examples encountered during the research.
The reason is simple.
If a researcher constructs a theory after observing a set of examples and then tests it on the same examples, the test cannot demonstrate independent support.
The researcher has effectively tested the theory against the evidence from which the theory was already built.
This is why QCROS requires explicit separation between construction, testing, and challenge.
When the corpus is too small to permit conventional separation, the system does not manufacture a false appearance of independence.
Instead, it may use alternative procedures such as sequential withholding or cross-testing. If genuine independent testing remains impossible, the claim may remain at rank C or D.
This principle lowers the claim rather than artificially raising the evidence.
XI. The QCROS Filter Architecture
The second chapter develops the operational filters through which a claim passes.
The architecture can be summarized as:
G / R / L
→ S
→ Q
→ X
→ I
→ Decision
The initial gates ask whether a proposed reading is linguistically and textually possible.
The later filters ask whether that possibility works in context, survives network testing, resists counterexamples, and rests on sufficiently independent evidence.
A crucial rule governs the system:
A later successful filter cannot compensate for a fatal failure in an earlier necessary condition.
This prevents what QCROS calls “filter compensation”: the practice of accumulating several positive indications in order to conceal one decisive defect.
XII. The G/R/L Possibility Gates
The G/R/L gates examine whether a proposed path is actually available before the researcher invests in broader interpretation.
They ask, in different ways:
Is the proposed graphical or textual form supported?
Is the reading legitimate within the relevant textual tradition or dataset?
Is the linguistic structure capable of carrying the proposed relation?
These gates do not answer:
“What is the correct meaning?”
They answer a more preliminary question:
“Is there a documented textual, reading, and linguistic path through which this claim can even be tested?”
A failed gate blocks the relevant path.
An unknown result suspends the judgment.
A successful result allows the claim to proceed to contextual examination.
XIII. The Context Filter — S
Passing the linguistic gates does not establish the local function of an expression.
The context filter therefore asks how the available possibilities behave in their actual textual environment.
Context may:
restrict a possible reading,
distinguish between competing functions,
support one relation over another,
or leave several possibilities open.
A successful S result means only that a hypothesis is viable within the specified context.
It does not yet establish a universal Qur’anic definition.
The distinction is crucial:
Context establishes local plausibility; network testing examines generalizability.
XIV. The Network Filter — Q
The network filter tests whether the proposed interpretation remains coherent when applied across the relevant corpus.
Its scope depends on the scope of the claim.
A local claim may require only a local network.
A claim about a grammatical form may require all relevant occurrences of that form.
A claim about a root or broader conceptual system requires a much larger corpus and a demonstrated justification for grouping the occurrences.
The network filter asks:
Does the proposed function recur?
Are there resistant cases?
Are the relationships genuine or merely thematic?
Is the same evidence being counted repeatedly?
Does the hypothesis require unexplained exceptions?
Can an alternative hypothesis explain the same data?
A Q “Pass” does not mean that the hypothesis is true.
It means that the hypothesis has not failed the specified network test.
XV. The Counterexample Filter — X
QCROS treats the search for counterexamples as a central part of research rather than as an optional final step.
A hypothesis that can explain every possible outcome is often impossible to falsify.
Therefore the researcher must identify in advance, where possible, what kind of observation would weaken or defeat the hypothesis.
This creates a methodological distinction between:
supporting evidence
and
survival under attempted refutation.
A hypothesis that survives deliberate attempts to falsify it has a different epistemic status from a hypothesis that has merely accumulated confirming examples.
The counterexample filter therefore asks:
What observation would count against this hypothesis?
If no answer can be given, the hypothesis may be too flexible to test.
XVI. Independence of Evidence — I
Evidence can appear stronger than it actually is when several pieces of evidence are derived from the same underlying observation.
QCROS therefore asks whether apparently separate evidences are genuinely independent.
The same clue cannot simply be counted multiple times under different labels.
For example, one textual association should not become:
a contextual argument,
a network argument,
a structural argument,
and a confirmation argument,
if all four are merely restatements of the same original observation.
The independence filter protects the research from double-counting.
XVII. The Decision Matrix
After the relevant filters have been completed, QCROS does not ask:
“Can we make this conclusion sound convincing?”
It asks:
“What is the highest claim that the evidence legitimately permits?”
The decision may produce:
A — Textually established within scope
B — Strong systemic result
C — Plausible but open hypothesis
D — Deferred
R — Rejected
The outcome is therefore not determined by the researcher’s preference.
It emerges from the documented path through which the claim was examined.
XVIII. The Twelve Operational Laws
The third chapter consolidates twelve operational laws into four families. These laws are not presented as new Qur’anic meanings. They are methodological rules governing how the research system itself operates.
Family One: Material Integrity
1. DATA-G1 — Separation of Raw Data from Analysis
Raw material must remain identifiable and protected from silent modification.
Corrections or transformations must be recorded separately.
The researcher may analyze the source, but must not rewrite the source and then treat the modified version as original data.
This protects the reproducibility of the research.
2. QAWLA-G1 — Stable Identity of the Textual Occurrence
The identity of a Qur’anic occurrence must remain stable even when its analysis changes.
The same textual unit may receive several competing analyses.
Changing the interpretation must not change the identity of the underlying textual record.
3. ORTHO-G1 — Separation of Orthographic Form and Interpretation
The written form of a textual occurrence and the researcher's interpretation of that form must remain distinct.
A feature of the writing system may be evidence for a hypothesis, but it cannot be silently transformed into the conclusion itself.
XIX. Family Two: Inference Control
4. SCOPE-G1 — Proportionality Between Claim and Corpus
The breadth of the claim must correspond to the breadth of the evidence.
A local observation cannot automatically become a universal law.
A claim about one expression cannot automatically become a claim about an entire root system.
The corpus must be appropriate to the scope of the statement.
5. RANK-G1 — Separation of Claim Layer and Claim Rank
The nature of a claim and the strength of its evidence are separate dimensions.
A researcher may propose a high-level hypothesis while assigning it a low rank.
The conceptual ambition of a claim must never determine its evidential rank.
6. GATE-G1 — No Compensation Between Filters
One successful test cannot cancel a necessary failure elsewhere.
A claim does not become acceptable merely because it has many positive features if one indispensable condition has failed.
The system therefore preserves path-specific failures rather than averaging them away.
XX. Family Three: Test Integrity
7. PARTITION-G1 — Separation of Construction, Testing, and Challenge
Evidence used to build a hypothesis must be distinguished from evidence used to test it.
Where possible, the challenge set should remain independent from the construction set.
This protects the research from data leakage and self-confirmation.
8. X-G1 — Falsifiability and Precommitment
A hypothesis must be exposed to possible failure.
The researcher should define, before examining the challenge material where possible, what kind of result would weaken or reject the hypothesis.
A hypothesis that changes silently whenever a counterexample appears is not genuinely being tested.
9. EVID-G1 — Evidence Independence and Anti-Double-Counting
Evidence derived from the same source event must not be counted repeatedly as though it were independent confirmation.
The purpose is to prevent evidential inflation.
XXI. Family Four: Governance and Closure
10. VERSION-G1 — Version Stability and No Silent Correction
Once a substantive claim has been tested, it cannot simply be rewritten without creating a new version.
The original version remains part of the historical record.
A later correction must identify what changed and whether the change requires renewed testing.
This rule protects the integrity of the research history.
11. CLOSED-G1 — Limited Closure and Conditions for Reopening
Closure applies to a defined claim, in a defined version, within a defined corpus and scope.
It does not mean that the subject has been permanently closed.
A new counterexample, a new corpus, a change in premises, or a methodological discovery may justify reopening the claim.
Thus:
Closure is controlled finality, not eternal finality.
12. MODEL-G1 — Separation of Model from Text and Independence of Reference
A model derived from Qur’anic research must not be equated with the Qur’anic text itself.
A model may be useful.
It may organize relationships.
It may generate operational procedures.
It may even perform successfully.
But operational success is not, by itself, proof of Qur’anic authorship or semantic identity.
The model must therefore declare:
its assumptions,
its mapping to the evidence,
its dependencies,
its operational performance,
and its semantic limitations.
This may be the most important protection in the entire architecture:
A successful model does not become Qur’anic merely because it works.
XXII. The Complete QCROS Cycle
The operational cycle can be represented simply:
1. Register the claim and its scope
↓
2. Identify and preserve the source and corpus
↓
3. Test textual, reading, and linguistic possibility
↓
4. Examine the local context
↓
5. Test the relevant network
↓
6. Search for counterexamples
↓
7. Test evidential independence
↓
8. Apply the decision matrix
↓
9. Assign the appropriate rank
↓
10. Close, defer, revise, or reject the claim
The cycle is not mechanically linear.
A network problem may send the researcher back to context.
A counterexample may require revision of the corpus.
An independence problem may invalidate an earlier test.
A new version may require the entire affected path to be rerun.
Every return must therefore leave a trace.
The goal is not to create a perfectly linear investigation.
The goal is to create an auditable investigation.
XXIII. Three Modes of Operation
QCROS provides different operating modes according to the scale of the research:
Compact Mode
Used for limited exploratory questions where the claim remains narrow.
Standard Mode
Used for ordinary structured research requiring documented corpus, hypotheses, tests, and ranking.
Full Mode
Used for major research projects requiring comprehensive auditability, independent testing, version control, challenge records, and complete methodological documentation.
The existence of different modes prevents the methodology from becoming unnecessarily heavy for every small question.
At the same time, a large claim cannot legitimately use a minimal procedure merely for convenience.
XXIV. What Does “Complete Research” Mean?
QCROS makes an important distinction between several concepts that are often confused.
A file can be complete without the evidence being strong.
A procedure can be correctly executed without the hypothesis succeeding.
A research package can be ready for a decision while the decision itself is D or R.
Therefore:
Research is complete when the conditions required to judge a defined claim have been completed—not when the hypothesis wins.
A complete investigation may legitimately end in:
A — established
B — strongly supported
C — still open
D — deferred
or
R — rejected.
This is one of the central philosophical commitments of QCROS.
The methodology does not define success as confirmation.
It defines success as epistemic integrity.
XXV. The Difference Between a Useful Model and a Qur’anic Claim
Modern research frequently produces models.
Models are useful because they simplify complex relationships and make them operational.
But simplification creates danger.
A researcher may discover a structural pattern in the Qur’anic corpus, translate that pattern into a conceptual diagram, develop an operational procedure from it, and eventually forget that the diagram is a human construction.
QCROS prevents this by maintaining three distinct identities:
Textual evidence
Human interpretation
Operational model
They may be connected.
They must not be conflated.
A model may therefore be evaluated on two separate dimensions:
semantic grounding
and
operational performance.
A model may perform well while having only a limited semantic claim.
Conversely, a strongly grounded interpretation may not yet have a useful operational model.
The distinction protects both scholarship and practical application.
XXVI. Why QCROS Matters Beyond Qur’anic Studies
Although QCROS was developed within Qur’anic linguistic research, its deeper methodological question is broader:
How can a researcher prevent a useful interpretation from becoming an unjustified certainty?
This question applies to many forms of knowledge.
It applies whenever:
evidence is incomplete,
models are constructed from observations,
researchers interpret patterns,
hypotheses are tested against data,
and human beings are tempted to identify their own conclusions with the reality they study.
The contribution of QCROS is therefore not a claim that human beings can eliminate error.
Its ambition is more modest and more practical:
Make the path to error visible.
If a conclusion is wrong, the methodology should help us discover where the error entered.
Was the corpus incomplete?
Was the text altered?
Was a root assumed rather than demonstrated?
Was a contextual clue generalized too quickly?
Was a counterexample ignored?
Was evidence counted twice?
Was a model mistaken for the text?
Was a revised hypothesis silently substituted for an earlier failed one?
The value of the system lies in making these questions auditable.
XXVII. The Ethical Dimension of Method
There is an ethical dimension to epistemology.
When a researcher presents a human hypothesis as though it were directly stated by God, the distinction between revelation and interpretation is weakened.
QCROS therefore treats epistemic humility not as a personal virtue only, but as a methodological requirement.
The researcher must be willing to say:
“The text states this.”
when the text states it.
“The corpus suggests this.”
when induction supports it.
“This is my hypothesis.”
when the researcher has constructed it.
“The hypothesis survived these tests.”
when it has been tested.
“The evidence is insufficient.”
when it is insufficient.
And:
“This hypothesis has been rejected.”
when a valid counterexample defeats it.
These distinctions protect the reference while allowing human inquiry to remain creative.
XXVIII. The First Volume’s Central Principle
The entire volume can be condensed into one methodological formula:
The text provides the material.
Induction reveals relationships.
The hypothesis proposes an explanation.
Testing determines whether the hypothesis survives.
Ranking determines what may legitimately be claimed.
The model transforms results into an operational tool without becoming identical to the text.
This sequence is the epistemic constitution of QCROS.
No stage should silently absorb the authority of another.
XXIX. From Volume One to Volume Two
The first volume stops at the point where knowledge becomes sufficiently governed to support responsible action.
The second volume takes the next step.
It asks what happens when a disciplined knowledge system enters the world of human action.
Human beings act within limits.
They possess different capacities.
They interact with other agents.
Their actions generate consequences.
Those consequences may differ from their intentions.
Outcomes may contain information that was not available before action.
Therefore the second volume moves toward:
responsibility
capacity
action
interaction
calibration
outcome
and limits of claim.
The key distinction remains:
Human beings can govern their action and improve their reading of outcomes; they cannot claim ownership of the total outcome.
This is why the second volume is called Engineering Support rather than “Engineering Victory.”
The project seeks to engineer what belongs to human responsibility.
It does not claim to engineer what lies beyond human control.
XXX. Conclusion: The Victory of Methodological Integrity
The first victory pursued by this project is not victory over another interpretation.
It is not victory over another school.
It is not even the victory of one theory over another.
It is the victory of disciplined inquiry over premature certainty.
The researcher does not become infallible through QCROS.
The text does not become mechanically reducible to a formula.
Interpretation does not disappear.
What changes is the researcher’s relationship with the interpretation.
A hypothesis becomes visibly a hypothesis.
A model becomes visibly a model.
A counterexample becomes valuable rather than threatening.
An unresolved question remains open rather than being buried under confident language.
And a human conclusion no longer needs to borrow the authority of the Qur’an in order to become intellectually valuable.
This is the deeper purpose of QCROS.
It seeks to establish a research culture in which the strongest statement is not always the most ambitious statement, but the statement whose scope corresponds to its evidence.
The researcher therefore ends not by saying:
“I have captured the final meaning.”
but by saying:
“Here is what the text provides. Here is what I observed. Here is what I inferred. Here is how I tested it. Here is what survived. Here is what failed. Here is what remains unknown. And here is the exact boundary between the Qur’anic reference and my own construction.”
That boundary is not a weakness.
It is the foundation of responsible knowledge.
A Short Reader’s Map
For the international reader, the entire volume can be remembered through ten questions:
1. What is the source?
Preserve the textual reference before interpreting it.
2. What exactly was observed?
Do not confuse observation with interpretation.
3. What is the claim?
Define its scope before attempting to prove it.
4. What is the hypothesis?
State the human construction explicitly.
5. What evidence supports it?
Identify the evidence rather than relying on rhetorical coherence.
6. What could disprove it?
A claim that cannot fail is difficult to test.
7. Was the test independent?
Do not test a hypothesis only against the evidence from which it was constructed.
8. What survived?
Separate successful tests from failed ones.
9. What rank does the evidence justify?
A, B, C, D, or R.
10. What remains outside the claim?
State the limits rather than hiding them.
This is the practical heart of QCROS.
Final Statement
QCROS is not a machine for producing certainty.
It is a system for controlling the production of claims.
It does not promise to eliminate human interpretation.
It seeks to make interpretation visible.
It does not promise that every question will receive an answer.
It creates a disciplined place for unanswered questions.
It does not turn models into revelation.
It keeps the reference above the model.
And it does not define research success as proving what the researcher hoped to prove.
It defines success as knowing, with increasing precision:
what the text says,
what the researcher observes,
what the researcher hypothesizes,
what the evidence permits,
what the tests eliminate,
what remains open,
and where human construction begins.
That is the methodological foundation upon which the second volume proceeds.
We engineer support and responsible action; we do not engineer victory itself.
