III. Executive Summary & Reader’s Guide
1. The Core Problematic This book confronts one of the most consequential methodological and theological crises in the history of Islamic thought: the traditional doctrine of al-Nāsikh wa al-Mansūkh (The Abrogating and the Abrogated), defined by medieval jurists as the "cancellation, erasure, nullification, or suspension of a Qur’anic ruling or recitation". Under the shadow of this post-prophetic construct, hundreds of definitive (Muḥkam) Qur’anic verses—particularly those establishing peace, forgiveness, freedom of conscience, mandatory parental bequests (Waṣiyyah), and social solidarity—were declared "operationally dead," retained in the Muṣḥaf merely for ritual recitation and blessing (Tabarruk) while stripped of their legislative authority. 

2. The Thesis & QCROS Epistemology Anchored in Epistemic Humility (al-Ḥayāʾ al-Maʿrifī) and the supreme authority of the Qur’anic text, this work demonstrates through lexical, contextual, and logical proof that the Qur’an is a book whose verses are perfected in precision and detailed by the All-Wise, All-Aware (Q 11:1). Falsehood cannot approach it from before it or from behind it (Q 41:42), and there is no changer of His words in this world or the Hereafter (Q 6:115; 18:27). 

3. QCROS Epistemic Coding System


• [N] Definite Textual Indication (Dalālah Naṣṣiyyah Qaṭʿiyyah): A ruling or meaning explicitly stated in the verse's wording, morphology, or direct syntax. Binding and non-negotiable.

• [C/I] Context-Governed Inference (Masār Istinbāṭī Maḥkūm bi-l-Siyāq): A conclusion derived from connecting a verse to its preceding and succeeding context, cross-referencing Qur’anic parallels, or applying rhetorical laws.

• [S/H] Structural Hypothesis (Faraḍiyyah Binyawiyyah): Insights from ʿUthmānic orthography (Rasm) and structural symmetry offered for reflection without claiming absolute finality.

• [RST-00] Reverse Stress Test (Ikhtibār al-Muqāwamah al-ʿAksī): Assuming the traditional abrogation claim as true for the sake of argument, then exposing its collision with explicit Qur’anic axioms, logic, and historical reality.

4. Glossary of Liberated Concepts


• Qur’anic Naskh vs. Traditional Maskh (Distortion): In the Qur’anic tongue, Naskh means verbatim transcription, documentation, confirmation, or detailed elucidation. The traditional notion of "nullification and erasure" is a later terminological deviation. 

• Topical Arrangement (al-Tartīl al-Mawḍiʿī): Moving a verse’s placement from its chronological order of revelation to its final topical position within a Sūrah by Divine command (the true meaning of Q 16:101).

• The Legislatively Unspoken (al-Maskūt ʿAnhu al-Tashrīʿī): Pre-Islamic or early customary practices upon which the Qur’an was initially silent (such as facing Jerusalem in prayer before Q 2:144, or consuming alcohol prior to prohibition), later addressed by an initial founding Qur’anic statute. Because no prior Qur’anic text commanded them, no Qur’anic verse was ever abrogated.

• Rotation of the Ruling with its Operative Cause (Dawarān al-Ḥukm maʿa al-Manāṭ): Two verses remaining simultaneously valid and active in the Muṣḥaf, each addressing a distinct human condition (e.g., strength vs. weakness, desire to remarry vs. desire to remain in the marital home). Each verse is activated whenever its specific condition (Manāṭ) materializes across history.

IV. Introduction: The Compass of Epistemic Humility (al-Ḥayāʾ al-Maʿrifī)
Approaching the Book of God requires more than lexical tools or memorized reports; it requires a governing spiritual and epistemological compass: Epistemic Humility (al-Ḥayāʾ al-Maʿrifī). This inner conscience instills awe before the speech of the Lord of the Worlds, preventing the researcher from daring to claim that God’s Book contains "nullified" or "cancelled" verses, that God issues a command in the morning only to revoke it a few hours later, or that a domestic goat entered a room and ate a parchment containing revealed Qur’an, causing it to vanish forever from the Muṣḥaf! 

1. The Collapse of the "Consensus" (Ijmāʿ) Myth [C/I] Proponents of abrogation have long wielded the sword of "scholarly consensus" (Ijmāʿ) to silence critical reflection. Yet an audit of the classical heritage itself exposes a chaotic numerical retreat that disproves any objective standard:


• Early extremists claimed 500 abrogated verses (claiming a single phrase in Q 9:5—the so-called "Verse of the Sword"—nullified over 120 to 200 verses of peace, patience, and forgiveness!).

• Ibn al-Jawzī (Nawāsikh al-Qurʾān) counted 247 (or 228) verses; Ibn Ḥazm counted 214; Ibn Salāmah counted 213; Ibn Barakāt counted 210; Makkī ibn Abī Ṭālib counted 200; al-Naḥḥās counted 134; and ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Baghdādī counted 66.

• Imām al-Suyūṭī (al-Itqān) reduced the entire list to 22 (or 20) verses. 

• Imām al-Shawkānī and Dr. Muṣṭafā Zayd (in his landmark Al-Azhar doctoral dissertation) reduced them to only 5 verses. 

• The QCROS Verdict: Even within those final 5 verses (and al-Suyūṭī’s 22), consensus is non-existent. Explicit disagreement is recorded inside Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī itself (where Ibn ʿUmar claims abrogation while Ibn ʿAbbās refutes it and affirms permanent validity!), alongside major classical authorities—such as Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, Abū Bakr ibn al-ʿArabī, Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim, and Ibn Kathīr—who defended their permanent precision (Iḥkām). 

2. The Total Absence of Explicit Prophetic Proof [N] A comprehensive survey of the canonical Ḥadīth corpus—the Six Books, the Nine, the Fourteen, and the Seventeen collections—yields a decisive fact: there is not a single authentic, explicit Marfūʿ report in which the Prophet ﷺ declared: "Verse X in Sūrah Y has been abrogated and its ruling is nullified; do not act upon it". What the tradition relied upon were either companion deductions where early scholars used the word Naskh in its broad pre-technical sense to mean "specification (Takhṣīṣ), exception (Istithnāʾ), or clarification (Bayān)"—which later jurists misconstrued as "total cancellation"—or solitary (Āḥād), anomalous fabrications that directly contradict the Qur’anic guarantee of preservation (Q 15:9). 

3. The Golden Rule of Jurisprudence [N] The Qur’an defines itself by two inseparable attributes: Structural Precision (Iḥkām) and Functional Detailing (Tafṣīl): "A Book whose verses are perfected in precision, then detailed from One All-Wise, All-Aware" (Q 11:1). Even classical legal theorists (Uṣūliyyūn) conceded the foundational maxim: "Whenever a verse rotates between the interpretation of permanent validity (Iḥkām) and abrogation (Naskh), it is mandatory to adopt permanent validity, because activating two proofs is superior to activating one and nullifying the other". 


V. Part One: Deconstructing the Traditional Foundation & The "Three Pillar Verses"
Advocates of nullifying abrogation rest their entire scriptural case on three primary verses: Q 2:106, Q 13:38–39, and Q 16:101. Subjecting these three verses to Qur’anic linguistic jurisprudence and contextual analysis proves that none of them refers to erasing Qur’anic verses or cancelling Islamic legislation. 


Chapter 1: The Qur’anic Lexicon of the Root (N-S-Kh) and Q 2:106
1. Exclusive Intra-Qur’anic Usage of (N-S-Kh): Documentation, Not Destruction [N] Outside the disputed verse (Q 2:106), the root N-S-Kh occurs in three decisive Qur’anic passages. In every single instance, it means recording, transcribing, documenting, and preserving an exact copy—never erasing or abolishing: 


• Q 22:52–53 (Sūrat al-Ḥajj): "Never did We send a messenger or a prophet before you, except that when he recited/aspired, Satan cast [doubts] into his recitation. Thereupon God transcribes/records (fa-yansakhu Allāhu) what Satan casts, then God establishes His verses in precision... so that He may make what Satan casts a trial (fitnah) for those in whose hearts is disease...". Reading verse 52 with the causal clause at the start of verse 53 ("so that He may make what Satan casts a trial") demolishes the traditional claim that yansakhu here means "erases". If God erased and obliterated what Satan cast, how could that obliterated thing remain an active trial (fitnah) and test for the sick-hearted and hard-hearted? The only coherent contextual meaning is that God documents and records the satanic fabrications and doubts as evidentiary exhibits, while establishing His clear verses against them, thereby sorting the sincere believer who clings to the Muḥkam Qur’an from the hypocrite who follows fabricated reports!

• Q 7:154 (Sūrat al-Aʿrāf): "...Moses took up the Tablets, and in their inscription/transcript (fī nuskhatihā) was guidance and mercy...". Here, Nuskhah is that which is inscribed and recorded on the Tablets. 

• Q 45:29 (Sūrat al-Jāthiyah): "This is Our Book speaking against you in truth. Indeed, We were transcribing/recording (nastansikhu) all that you used to do". 

2. Liberating Q 2:106 ("Mā nansakh min āyatin aw nunsihā...") [N / C/I] "Whatever sign/verse We transcribe [or supersede in miracle] or delay/cause to be forgotten, We bring one better than it or similar to it. Do you not know that God has power over all things?" (Q 2:106). The precision of this verse unfolds through two complementary analytical tracks: 


• Track A — Naskh as Transcription and Detailed Elucidation: Based on the Qur’anic root N-S-Kh, "Mā nansakh min āyatin" means: whatever legislative sign We transcribe, confirm, and detail—or whatever We record in the Qur’an from previous divine scriptures—"aw nunsihā" (or whatever We defer in its detailed exposition to an appointed time, or omit from the exhaustive histories of past nations), "We bring one better than it" (offering broader legislative options and fuller detail suited to diverse human states) "or similar to it" (a confirming elucidation equivalent to the original principle, in accordance with Q 41:43: "Nothing is said to you except what was already said to the messengers before you").

• Track B — Āyah as Cosmic Miracle and Evidentiary Sign: Leading rhetoricians and exegetes—including Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, Muḥammad ʿAbduh, Rashīd Riḍā (Tafsīr al-Manār), the Al-Azhar Committee (al-Muntakhab), and Abū Muslim al-Iṣfahānī—established that Āyah here denotes cosmic miracles authenticating prophets, supported by three internal contextual proofs:


1. The Rhetorical Closure ("God has power over all things" — ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr): Rhetoric demands matching the divine attribute to the subject matter. If the verse concerned replacing one legal rule with another, it would conclude with "All-Knowing, All-Wise" (ʿAlīmun Ḥakīm), as legislation pertains to wisdom and knowledge. Concluding with Omnipotent Power (Qudrah) and Cosmic Sovereignty (Mulk al-Samāwāt wa-l-Arḍ) in v. 107 points directly to physical miracles and cosmic signs! 

2. The Immediate Sequel (Q 2:108): "Or do you wish to ask your Messenger as Moses was asked before?". The context addresses the obstinacy of the People of the Book and polytheists who demanded tailored physical miracles like those of Moses. God responds that He has the power to supersede a temporary sensory miracle given to a past prophet, or let it fade by the passage of time, and bring through the Final Messenger a better miracle (the rational, eternal, linguistic miracle of the Qur’an) or a similar sensory sign.

3. The Logical Four-Possibility Matrix: If Naskh meant "cancellation" and Mā were conditional, the claim "Any verse We cancel, We bring one identical to it (mithlihā)" would be an exercise in futility! Why would the All-Wise cancel a verse only to reveal its exact duplicate? Furthermore, if the traditionalists built an entire discipline called "The Science of the Abrogating and Abrogated" (ʿIlm al-Nāsikh wa-l-Mansūkh) from the first verb (nansakh), why did they never invent a "Science of the Forgetting and Forgotten" (ʿIlm al-Nāsī wa-l-Mansī) from the adjacent verb (aw nunsihā)? Their selectivity betrays that "abrogation" was weaponized to suspend verses of peace, freedom, and bequests. 

Chapter 2: Liberating Q 13:38–39, Q 16:101, and Q 87:6–7
1. Q 13:38–39 ("God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book") [N] Traditionalists severed the clause "God erases what He wills and confirms" from the beginning of the passage to claim God erases Qur’anic rulings. Reading the full unit restores its harmony: "And it was not for any messenger to bring a sign/miracle (āyah) except by God’s permission. For every appointed term there is a decree (li-kulli ajalin kitāb). God erases what He wills and confirms..." (Q 13:38–39). The opening explicitly defines Āyah as the miracles of messengers. Every era has its suitable divine sign: God terminates the temporary sensory miracle of a previous prophet when his era ends, and confirms the eternal miracle of the Qur’an for the final message.

2. Q 16:101 ("And when We substitute a verse in place of a verse [baddalnā āyatan makāna āyatin]...") [N] Three decisive proofs refute using this verse for legal abrogation:


• Chronological Proof (Meccan Revelation): Sūrat al-Naḥl is indisputably Meccan. Detailed legal statutes, penal codes, and transactional laws were revealed later in Medina. How could a Meccan verse speak of abrogating legal rulings that had not yet been revealed? 

• Contextual Proof (Q 16:102): The following verse states: "Say: The Holy Spirit has brought it down from your Lord in truth, to make firm those who believe...". Diverse signs and arguments are revealed to strengthen believers, not to confuse them with self-cancelling laws.

• Lexical Precision (Topical Arrangement / al-Tartīl al-Mawḍiʿī): The Qur’an says "substitute a verse in the place of a verse" (makāna āyah). This describes shifting a verse’s spatial placement from its chronological order of descent to its final topical location within the Sūrah by divine instruction. Moving a verse’s position within the Book proves its retention inside the Book—for if God wanted to cancel it, He would have deleted it rather than relocated it! 

3. Q 87:6–7 ("We shall make you recite, so you shall not forget, except what God wills") [N] Traditionalists used the exception "except what God wills" to justify reports attributed to Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (Muslim 1050) claiming entire Sūrahs as long as Barāʾah were forgotten from memory, leaving behind only stylistically weak sentences ("If the son of Adam had two valleys of wealth..."). In truth, had the Prophet ﷺ forgotten legislative Sūrahs that were lost from the Muṣḥaf, the religion would be incomplete (contradicting Q 5:3 and Q 16:89). Rather, the verse assures the Prophet ﷺ that because the revelation was immediately transcribed and documented in writing during his lifetime, he was relieved of the anxiety of natural human forgetfulness; any momentary lapse in human memory was safeguarded by the written preservation between the two covers.


Chapter 3: The Supremacy of the Qur’an, the Fall of "Substitution Reports," and the Internal Collapse of Classical Uṣūl
1. Substitution (Tabdīl) is a Rejected Polytheistic Demand (Q 10:15) [N] The Qur’an records the exact demand of those who rejected the revelation: "And when Our clear verses are recited to them, those who do not expect to meet Us say: 'Bring a Qur’an other than this, or change/substitute it (aw baddilhu).' Say: 'It is not for me to change it of my own accord. I only follow what is revealed to me...'" (Q 10:15). God locked the door against textual or legislative cancellation with an absolute seal:


• "And the Word of your Lord has been fulfilled in truth and in justice. None can change His Words (lā mubaddila li-kalimātihi)" (Q 6:115; see also Q 18:27; Q 10:64; Q 50:29). What the polytheists failed to extract from the Prophet ﷺ during his lifetime by explicit Qur’anic prohibition, fabricated reports attributed to Companions after his death attempted to establish under the guise of "abrogation of recitation":

• The Myth of the Domestic Goat (al-Dājin): The report attributing to ʿĀʾishah that a verse of ten (then five) adult sucklings was written on a sheet under her bed, and while the household was preoccupied with the Prophet’s ﷺ death, a goat entered and ate the sheet, yet it remained among what was recited as Qur’an!

• The Myth of the Missing Stoning Verse (Āyat al-Rajm): The claim that a verse reading "The older man and older woman, if they fornicate, stone them definitely" was revealed, recited, and then erased in wording while its lethal penal ruling remained active without a single word in the Muṣḥaf! As Muḥammad ʿAbduh and Rashīd Riḍā declared in Tafsīr al-Manār: "Among the definitive signs of a fabricated Ḥadīth is its contradiction of conclusive rational or scriptural proof and the foundations of creed—chief among which is that not a single letter of the Qur’an was altered or dropped". 

2. Internal Admissions of Classical Legal Theorists (Uṣūliyyūn) [C/I] An examination of the foundational manuals of Uṣūl al-Fiqh (al-Samarqandī’s Mīzān al-Uṣūl, al-Āmidī’s al-Iḥkām, al-Zarkashī’s al-Burhān, and al-Zarqānī’s Manāhil al-ʿIrfān) reveals fatal internal contradictions that dismantle their own theory:


• Al-Samarqandī’s Admission of "Illusion": In Mīzān al-Uṣūl (2/978), al-Samarqandī defines Naskh as: "The clarification of the end of an absolute legal ruling, whose continuation was merely in the estimation of our illusions (fī taqdīri awhāminā) had it not been for the clarifying text". Notice the confession: "in the estimation of our illusions"! God did not cancel His law; rather, the jurist mistakenly imagined a conditional or context-specific ruling to be absolute, and when another verse clarified its boundary, the jurist called the correction of his own illusion "abrogation of the Qur’an"!

• Violation of Their Own Four Conditions: Classical theorists stipulated that Naskh requires (a) chronological separation (Tarākhī) between the two texts, and (b) an absolute, irreconcilable contradiction (Taʿāruḍ Ḥaqīqī) from every angle. Yet in practice, they claimed abrogation between consecutive verses revealed in the exact same breath and passage (such as Q 8:65 and 66; Q 58:12 and 13; Q 2:284 and 286) where no contradiction exists once pronouns and prepositions are respected!

• Al-Bāqillānī’s Rejection of Solitary Reports (Āḥād): Classical manuals explicitly admit regarding "abrogation of recitation": "Many scholars rejected this category because its proof rests on solitary reports (Akhbār Āḥād) subject to doubt; and as Judge Abū Bakr al-Bāqillānī recorded in al-Intiṣār, it is impermissible to establish the revelation or abrogation of the Qur’an via solitary reports that carry no conclusive authority".

3. Jurisprudence of the "Unspoken" (al-Maskūt ʿAnhu) & Therapeutic Gradualism [N]


• The Change of Qiblah (Q 2:144): Traditionalists claim Q 2:144 abrogated praying toward Jerusalem. Yet there is not a single verse in the Qur’an commanding prayer toward Jerusalem in the first place! The Prophet’s initial orientation toward Jerusalem belonged to the "Legislatively Unspoken" (al-Maskūt ʿAnhu)—following the custom of prior prophets to purge the Companions’ hearts of pre-Islamic nationalist attachment to the idol-filled Kaʿbah. Once their hearts were purified for pure monotheism, the Qur’an revealed its initial founding statute directing prayer toward the Sacred Mosque. No Qur’anic verse ever abrogated another.

• The Alcohol Verses (Q 2:219; Q 4:43; Q 5:90): The Qur’an never permitted alcohol in any verse only to cancel it later. Rather, addressing an entrenched pre-Islamic addiction with the wisdom of a Divine Physician:


1. Q 2:219 ("In them is great sin and some benefits for people, but their sin is greater than their benefit") is a declarative factual statement and an axiological scale establishing the permanent legal maxim that "repelling harms takes precedence over securing benefits". Declarative statements of fact (Akhbār) can never be abrogated! 

2. Q 4:43 ("Do not approach prayer while intoxicated until you know what you are saying") remains permanently valid as the foundational condition of mental consciousness and sobriety in prayer (applicable to anesthesia, narcotics, or extreme drowsiness).

3. Q 5:90 ("...so avoid it...") sealed the decisive prohibition. All three verses remain permanently active (Muḥkam) within their respective domains.

Chapter 4: Exclusive Legislative Sovereignty & The Theological Danger of "Legislative Associating"
God declared His exclusive legislative prerogative in unambiguous terms: "Legislation belongs to none but God" (Q 12:40); "And He shares not His legislation with anyone" (Q 18:26); "Shall I seek other than God as a judge, when He is the One who has revealed to you the Book explained in detail (mufaṣṣalan)?" (Q 6:114); "Today I have perfected your religion for you" (Q 5:3).

When medieval compilations of al-Nāsikh wa-l-Mansūkh claimed the authority to suspend hundreds of definitive Qur’anic commands based on human opinions or unverified solitary reports, they fell into grave theological perils condemned by the Qur’an:


1. Believing in Part of the Book and Rejecting Part (Q 2:85): Saying "We recite the verse of parental bequest (Q 2:180) or religious freedom (Q 2:256) for blessing, but its legal ruling is abolished and forbidden to apply" is the practical equivalent of believing in the utterance while rejecting its legislative authority.

2. Turning the Scripture into Concealed Parchments (Q 6:91): "You make it into parchments that you display, while you conceal much"—preserving the calligraphy of the verses while burying their social and legal operation.

3. Unauthorized Legislative Associating (Q 42:21): "Or do they have partners who have legislated for them of religion that which God has not authorized?".

VI. Part Two: The Applied Field — Deconstructing the "Five Consensus Verses" and al-Suyūṭī’s 22-Verse Canon
Chapter 1: The Architecture of Inner Accountability & Moral Capacity (Q 2:284–286) [N]
• The Traditional Claim: Q 2:284 ("Whether you disclose what is within yourselves or conceal it, God will bring you to account for it") was allegedly abrogated by Q 2:286 ("God does not burden any soul beyond its capacity").

• The Linguistic & Contextual Proof of Iḥkām:


1. Direct Connection to Concealing Testimony (Q 2:283): Verse 284 immediately follows the command: "And do not conceal testimony; for whoever conceals it, his heart is indeed sinful". Concealing testimony is a deliberate, volitional act of the heart fully within human capacity! 

2. Centrality of Intentions in Islam: Hypocrisy (Nifāq), ostentation (Riyāʾ), malice, and premeditated fraud are all concealed inner states. If inner accountability were abrogated, the foundational principle "Actions are judged by intentions" would collapse!

3. Clarification, Not Cancellation: As Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Manār established, when some Companions feared they would be punished for involuntary, fleeting mental whispers (Khawāṭir Qahriyyah), verse 286 descended to clarify and specify verse 284: a soul is held accountable only for what its heart deliberately earns and resolves upon (mā kasabat), which lies within its capacity (wusʿahā), whereas involuntary passing thoughts are pardoned.

Chapter 2: The Morphological Engineering of Sexual Offenses (Q 4:15–16 & Q 24:2) [N]
• The Traditional Claim: Q 4:15–16 originally prescribed house confinement and verbal/corporal reprimand for heterosexual adultery (Zinā), and were subsequently abrogated by the flogging verse in Q 24:2 (and the alleged stoning report).

• The Morphological & Prepositional Proof of Iḥkām: In Arabic grammar, pronouns and relative nouns are mathematically exact:


1. Q 4:15 — Female Homosexuality / Lesbianism (al-Siḥāq): "And those women who (wa-llātī — exclusive feminine plural!) commit lewdness among your women, bring four witnesses against them from among you; if they testify, confine them to the houses until death takes them or God appoints for them (lahunna) a way out".


• Morphological Proof: Allātī is an exclusive feminine plural (three or more females); it can never in the Arabic tongue refer to a man and a woman! As the master exegete Mujāhid ibn Jabr explicitly stated (supported by al-Rāzī, al-Manār, and al-Shaʿrāwī), this verse legislates the preventive and rehabilitative measure for female-to-female sexual misconduct (Siḥāq): isolating them within the home to sever corrupting female networks.

• Prepositional Precision (Lahunna vs. ʿAlayhinna): God said "or God appoints for them (lahunna) a way" using the preposition Lām of benefit, mercy, and relief (i.e., repentance and natural marriage)—He did not say "against them" (ʿalayhinna)! Had the "way" been flogging and stoning to death—as the traditionalists claimed—God would have said ʿalayhinna, because a punishment is inflicted against the offender, not for her benefit!

2. Q 4:16 — Male Homosexuality / Sodomy (al-Liwāṭ): "And the two males who (wa-lladhāni — exclusive masculine dual!) commit it among you, discipline/chastise them both (fa-ādhūhumā); but if they repent and reform, leave them alone...". Following the feminine plural in v. 15, the masculine dual Alladhāni designates male-to-male sexual misconduct (Liwāṭ) (which occurs between two males), assigning discretionary judicial deterrence (Taʿzīr) until repentance and behavioral reform are achieved.

3. Q 24:2 — Heterosexual Fornication (al-Zinā): "The fornicatress and the fornicator (al-zāniyatu wa-l-zānī — singular feminine + singular masculine), flog each one of them with a hundred lashes...".

o Conclusion: Rather than two cancelled verses and a missing stoning verse, the Qur’an provides a complete, non-overlapping tripartite penal architecture covering all three distinct offenses (female homosexuality in Q 4:15, male homosexuality in Q 4:16, and heterosexual fornication in Q 24:2), as documented by Abū Bakr ibn al-ʿArabī in Aḥkām al-Qurʾān.

Chapter 3: Prophetic Matrimonial Covenants — Past Confirmation vs. Future Restriction (Q 33:50 & 52) [N]
• The Traditional Claim: Q 33:50 ("O Prophet, We have made lawful to you your wives to whom you have given their dowries...") and Q 33:52 ("Women are not lawful to you hereafter [min baʿdu], nor that you should exchange them for other wives...") contradict and abrogate one another (forcing traditionalists to claim v. 50 abrogated v. 52, even though v. 50 precedes v. 52!).

• The Temporal & Syntactic Proof of Iḥkām:


1. Q 33:50 Governs the Existing Past: When the four-wife limit was legislated for believers in general, men with more than four wives were required to divorce the excess. Because the Prophet’s ﷺ existing wives—expressed in the past tense "to whom you have given (ātayta) their dowries"—were declared Mothers of the Believers who could never marry anyone else after him (Q 33:53), divorcing any of them would have condemned her to lifelong deprivation.
