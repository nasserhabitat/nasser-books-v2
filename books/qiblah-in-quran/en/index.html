Opening of the book

The central discipline of the original work is also preserved: evidence is not the same as interpretation; interpretation is not the same as hypothesis; and a hypothesis is not a law simply because it is attractive or comprehensive. This condensed edition therefore aims to remain intellectually open while still giving the foreign reader a coherent view of the book’s main discovery.

Qiblah (القبلة) is usually presented as one of the clearest concepts in Islamic religious consciousness: the direction toward which Muslims turn in prayer. This is true as far as it goes. The Qur’an itself repeatedly commands the turning of the face toward al-Masjid al-Haram (المسجد الحرام). Yet the book asks whether this spatial description exhausts the Qur’anic function of Qiblah.

The question arises because the Qur’anic passages do more than identify a direction. In the surrounding textual network, Qiblah appears beside the face, turning, transformation, al-Masjid al-Haram, the house, east and west, righteousness, guidance, following, trial, the middle community, witnessing, and prayer. The existence of such a network does not prove that all these ideas belong to the definition of Qiblah. But it does require the researcher to explain why they appear where they do and what kind of relationship each one has to the concept.
