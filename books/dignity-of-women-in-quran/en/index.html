II. Translator’s Note: The Bridge of Meaning

This English edition is a condensed conceptual adaptation, not a word-for-word translation of the Arabic original.

Its purpose is to make the book’s central argument, methodology, evidence structure, and principal conclusions accessible to readers who may not possess a background in Arabic, Qur’anic studies, or Islamic jurisprudence.

The Arabic edition remains the full reference text.

Whenever the original work distinguishes between what is established, inferred, hypothesized, proposed, or still open to investigation, this English edition preserves that distinction. Compression must not turn a hypothesis into a conclusion, an interpretive possibility into a Qur’anic statement, or a methodological proposal into a religious ruling.

Key Qur’anic terms are therefore retained, where necessary, in transliteration followed by an explanatory English equivalent. The purpose is not merely linguistic accuracy. It is to preserve the conceptual architecture of the original inquiry.

This edition is consequently a bridge of meaning: shorter than the Arabic work, but intended to remain faithful to its epistemic boundaries.

III. Introduction

Human Dignity and the Responsibility of Reading

This book began with a question about women:

How should the rights, actions, responsibilities, and relationships of women be read within the Qur’an?

The inquiry soon revealed that no image of woman exists in isolation. Every construction of woman implicitly constructs man as well.

If woman is defined as permanently dependent, man may consequently be imagined as permanently self-sufficient. If woman is reduced to her body, man may in turn be reduced to provision, authority, strength, or control.

The deeper problem is therefore not simply how a tradition, society, or interpreter describes women.

The more fundamental question is:

By what standard do we evaluate the human being?

The Qur’an declares:

“We have certainly honored the children of Adam.”

(Qur’an 17:70)

Elsewhere, it addresses men and women in relation to belief, action, responsibility, and recompense. At the same time, it contains differences in certain rights, obligations, family relationships, financial distributions, and procedures.

This book does not solve the apparent tension by erasing either side.

It asks instead:

Does a difference in function, responsibility, procedure, or entitlement necessarily establish a difference in human worth?

The central methodological answer developed throughout this study is:

Difference does not interpret itself.

A difference established in one domain remains a difference in that domain unless additional evidence justifies transferring it into a universal judgment about the human value of a person or an entire sex.

IV. What This Book Claims — and What It Does Not

This work does not claim to be a comprehensive commentary on the Qur’an.

It does not present a new school of Islamic jurisprudence.
